Erdős and Gyárfás asked whether every coloring of the edges of a complete graph on r² + 1 vertices with r colors has r + 1 vertices that miss a color. Our Lean proof puts every possible exception below 255 · 2136. The goal is a proof for every r ≥ 3.

The paper’s theorem

2144

The manuscript proves the conjecture for every number of colors r ≥ 2144. This is the smallest power of two covered by the argument.

Sharpest cutoff in this project

255 · 2136

The same proof works slightly below 2144. Each of five inequalities had room to spare. Using that margin lowers the cutoff by exactly 1/256, or 0.390625%.

The corollary leaves every width comparison positive. The smallest certified margin is greater than 0.00015316. The paper checks 1,280 endpoints. Lean uses a separate subdivision and proves the inequality throughout all 45 intervals.

Research history

From five colors to a finite range.

I started with five colors. Could I find six vertices that miss a color in every coloring of the 325 edges on 26 vertices? In July I used OpenAI models to work through that case, then six, seven, eight and nine.

1999
Erdős and Gyárfás published the conjecture and proved it for three and four colors. Original paper
20–21 July 2026
I released my proof manuscripts for five through eight colors on July 20 and for nine on July 21. Ramazan Kara separately released a machine verification of the five-color case that credits my proof. Five through eight · Nine · Kara’s verification

Hai Xia and Stefan Szeider posted a separate computational proof for five colors. Their October 17 version does not cite my July release. Both dated accounts are linked here so readers can see the chronology. October paper, version 1
November 2026
Our November paper gives a separate argument at twelve colors. The large-color theorem has an end-to-end Lean proof. Full manuscript

Work toward the full conjecture

What remains open?

13 ≤ r < 255 · 2136

This is the range left by the project’s manuscripts. At the lower end, thirteen colors means 170 vertices. The target is to find fourteen that miss a color. At the upper end, lowering the cutoff to 2143 would require a stronger estimate: one of the five current width comparisons already fails there.

What Lean checks today

For every color count at or above the cutoff, Lean proves that any coloring of the complete graph on r² + 1 vertices has r + 1 vertices missing a color. The downloadable source includes the theorem, exact arithmetic and replay instructions. Independent mathematical review of the paper is pending.
