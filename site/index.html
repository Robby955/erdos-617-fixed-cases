A finite cutoff for balanced colorings

Robert Sneiderman and OpenAI GPT-6.2 Astra

Color every edge of a complete graph on r² + 1 vertices with r colors. Erdős and Gyárfás asked whether some r + 1 vertices must miss a color.

We prove this for every r ≥ 2143. The proof is formalized in Lean.

Read the paper PDFLean theorem

The proof

Suppose every set of r + 1 vertices sees every color. Fix a color with the fewest edges. Most of its edges lie within parts of a partition. We extract cliques of that color and seek one vertex from each, with enough vertices outside the cliques to reach r + 1. At the stated cutoff, the edges needed to block every such choice exceed the number available.

Appendix J proves the cutoff by counting the edges lost in a third pruning pass. Lean, Lean4Lean and Nanoda accepted the same proof export. Independent mathematical review of the paper is pending.

Open cases

The fixed-case manuscripts and the cutoff leave 13 ≤ r < 2143. The first remaining case asks whether every thirteen-coloring on 170 vertices has fourteen vertices that miss a color.

Research history

I began with the five-color case in July 2026. I wanted to find six vertices missing a color in every coloring of the edges on 26 vertices. Working with OpenAI models, I went on to six, seven, eight and nine colors.
