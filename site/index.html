A finite cutoff for balanced colorings

Robert Sneiderman and OpenAI GPT-6.2 Astra

Color every edge of a complete graph on r² + 1 vertices with r colors. Erdős and Gyárfás asked whether some r + 1 vertices must miss a color.

We prove this for every r ≥ 255 · 2136. The proof is formalized in Lean.

Read the paper PDFLean theorem

The proof

Suppose every set of r + 1 vertices sees every color. Choose the color with the fewest edges. Most of its edges lie in rows, and inside those rows we find cliques. We try to choose one vertex from each clique, then add enough outside vertices to reach r + 1. Each blocked choice costs edges. At large orders those costs exceed the number available.

Theorem A gives the simpler bound r ≥ 2144. Appendix I sharpens it by a factor of 255/256 and follows the final contradiction into Lean. Lean, Lean4Lean and Nanoda accepted the same proof export. Independent mathematical review of the paper is pending.

Open cases

The fixed-case manuscripts and the cutoff leave 13 ≤ r < 255 · 2136. The first remaining case asks whether every thirteen-coloring on 170 vertices has fourteen vertices that miss a color.

Research history

I started with five colors in July 2026, looking for six vertices that miss a color among the 325 edges on 26 vertices. Working with OpenAI models, I went on to six, seven, eight and nine colors.
